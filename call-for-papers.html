Topic, motivation, and relevance

Why this workshop now

Large language models, recommender systems, and multimodal agents are no longer just external tools. They increasingly mediate personal awareness, preferences, memory, reflection, and identity formation.

Yet the scholarly conversation on AI and selfhood has remained concentrated in normative ethics and philosophy. What is still missing is computational and empirical infrastructure: ways to model preference shift, track longitudinal changes in self-representation, and evaluate when systems reshape rather than merely reflect user values.

AI and the Self aims to bring these questions into the core of machine learning research by treating self-dynamics as something that can be studied, measured, and designed for in coupled human-AI systems.

Workshop scope

Interdisciplinary by design

This workshop connects machine learning, NLP, HCI, cognitive science, psychology, philosophy, sociology, education, healthcare, and ethics.

We are especially interested in work that bridges technical methods with empirical, cultural, social, or conceptual analysis.

Themes

Workshop themes

The workshop is organized around five linked themes connecting technical AI research with empirical, social, cultural, and philosophical inquiry.

Measurement and evaluation

How can we measure AI’s effects on self-concept, agency, confidence, dependence, identity formation, and skill change?

Technical design and interaction

How should AI systems implement memory, personalization, uncertainty, abstention, refusal, and “not knowing” without encouraging over-agreement or unhealthy attachment?

Culture, language, and worldview

How do AI systems encode assumptions about the self, intelligence, emotion, and agency, and how can they better support diverse identities?

Professional, educational, and epistemic transformation

How does AI change learning, authorship, expertise, creativity, classroom culture, and professional identity?

Philosophical, ethical, legal, and spiritual foundations

How should autonomy, responsibility, consciousness, authorship, and personhood be understood when self-expression is co-created with AI?

Submissions

What we invite

Technical work

Model design, personalization, memory, uncertainty, abstention, interaction design, and evaluation.

Empirical studies

AI-mediated reflection, development, education, dependence, identity formation, and longitudinal human-AI interaction.

Conceptual work

Selfhood, agency, authenticity, responsibility, consciousness, authorship, culture, and personhood.

Applications and demos

Education, mental health, physical health, avatars, reflective systems, and cultural-preservation tools.

Format

Submission notes

  • Short and long non-archival papers.
  • Technical, empirical, socio-cultural, philosophical, and application-oriented perspectives are all welcome.
  • Work in progress, early findings, and new problem framings are encouraged.
  • Submissions must be unpublished and not under review elsewhere.
  • Work accepted to the main conference may not be presented here.
